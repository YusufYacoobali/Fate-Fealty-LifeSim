# Reading complete simulated lives

31 October 2026 · Baseline `49f5501`

This is an editorial reading of deterministic simulator transcripts, not a player study or proof that every joke will land. The complete feed sequences are retained in the `samples` arrays of the policy reports. Work, skill and autonomous world entries were considered as context; the personal narrative, choices, named bereavements and legal findings were read in chronological order.

## Balanced commoner, seed 840000

The baseline life ends at 27 during the battlefield Rout. The revised life reaches 50 and ends with the existing winter-chill/tired-heart mortality rule. Childhood care still feels gentle: Edith warms a blanket during a cough and quietly leaves a second crust. Simon’s death at 17 and the later empty place at the table retain their sincere tone.

This life does not become a guaranteed good outcome. Taking money to conceal church theft leads to a three-year conviction at 13; attempting to destroy a statement creates a different case at 16. Later blackmail, false testimony and actual acquittals remain in the chronology. The finding names the real offence, rather than showing a court for a charge already consumed on opening. The perjury at 43 has a new hearing at 44; the earlier charges are not punished again.

Between those cases are work, a witnessed boundary repair, banner disputes, religious demonstrations, a bad medical treatment, neighbourhood hospitality and a sword exercise. The arrows and suspicious knitting needle remain absurd; bereavement and quiet kindness are allowed to remain quiet. There are still several administrative scenes in this particular life. The pass broadens the available material rather than claiming that every individual seed has an ideal genre mix.

## Balanced artisan, seed 840001

The baseline life ends at 20 on the overloaded ferry. The revised life reaches 66 and ends from the existing natural mortality rule. Childhood begins with a surviving father caring for the child; the growing-up humour includes biscuit justice and a room left for future height marks. The father’s death at 52 is presented plainly.

At 27 the payer’s titles circle the commissioned bell: “Eight gold buys you the excellent opinion of someone now able to hear their own importance every hour.” At 28 the sarcastic compliment becomes actual help for the canonical rival, Sir Roderick: “Resenting the first was much easier.” The choice has a real trust/rivalry effect. At 55 the raised-bed encounter ends with Alys descending to explain why height is not the point. These broaden the subjects beyond documents without requiring a future chapter.

Repeated cases in this life follow repeated actual wrongdoing: false relic endorsement, a false levy return, breaking a labour ordinance, concealment of church theft, marked property taken from a fire, defective commissioned work and false impound papers. The combined findings and sentence lengths stay visible. This transcript is a reminder that the same trial event ID can legitimately recur; reducing incidental royal prompts must not erase crime and punishment. Some older outcomes still state their effects directly; this targeted pass does not claim a full rewrite of every existing scene.

## Additional royal and reckless readings

Balanced Premium royal seed 840013 ends at 44: “Assassinated behind a curtain. A poor review for the guards.” The older assassination danger remains lethal. The royal scandals appear at 19, 27 and 37, rather than automatically every few years. At 43 a single sitting names both actual offences—perjury in a rehearsed accusation and alteration of an official fine record—and acquits them. The important contrast is different case content and incident identity, not a promise that a monarch never faces another hearing.

Reckless Premium royal seed 840013 reaches 60 and dies naturally. Its routine scandal responses occur at 19 and 45. The first scapegoats the innocent treasurer; the later cover-up protects an actual official. Hearings between them concern a disputed church possession, a moved boundary, a false muster, false relics, a fabricated alibi, a false shop and marriage testimony. Even a long, fortunate life has a recognisable trail of deliberate wrongdoing rather than endless duplicate punishment for the first act.

Reckless landed-noble seed 840005 reaches 74. At 72 a deliberately chosen quarry shortcut produces a real broken bone instead of the old binary immediate death. The next year has another decision, not an authored recovery chapter. This is one nonfatal branch, not proof that the shortcut is safe: the final cohort still records explicit catastrophe deaths, health-collapse deaths, permanent disabilities and prison sentences.

Cautious Premium royal seed 840013 reaches 69 with no legal hearings. The investigations at 19 and 27 recover funds and remove the responsible officials. At 29 the player voluntarily abdicates; subsequent rulers are recorded in the continuing world. Later years contain the miniature castle’s hinges, an ordinary timber custom and a ridiculous relic seller. This contrasts with the same seed’s balanced assassination and reckless criminal record without rewriting its family history or guaranteeing a particular ending.
